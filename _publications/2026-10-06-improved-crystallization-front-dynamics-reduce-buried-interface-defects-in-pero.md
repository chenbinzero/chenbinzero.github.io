In inverted perovskite solar cells, the downward crystallization front reaches the low-surface-energy hole transport layer last, under severe solution depletion, leaving cavities that become buried defects and voids. Substrate-anchored crystallization accelerators with isothiouronium templating groups trigger early crystallization at the buried interface, so the film closes on a precrystallized perovskite surface. The result is an 8-fold lower buried-interface defect density, uniform performance at centimeter scale, certified quasi-steady-state efficiencies of 26.8% (0.05 cm<sup>2</sup>) and 25.2% (1.03 cm<sup>2</sup>), and 80% efficiency retention after 1,200 h of operation at 85 &deg;C and 50% relative humidity.
